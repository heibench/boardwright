# 03 tps54202_buck

**Reference:** TI TPS54202 datasheet SLVSD26C, Figure 7-1 "5V, 2A Reference
Design" and section 7.4.1 layout guidelines. Read from the PDF this time:
C1 10 uF, C2 0.1 uF, C3 0.1 uF boot, L1 15 uH, C4/C5 22 uF, R1 49.9, R2 100k,
R3 13.3k, C6 75 pF, R4 511k, R5 105k. Vref 0.596 V gives 5.08 V.
Chosen here, not from TI: footprints, the inductor series (Bourns SRN6045TA
as a candidate; check its saturation current against 2 A plus ripple before
ordering), terminal blocks, the power LED, mounting holes.

**Stresses:** a layout where the autorouter's answer is electrically legal and
wrong. Wide current paths, a hot switching loop, a feedback node to keep quiet.

**What broke**
1. Power nets at 0.6 mm: Freerouting left SW and VOUT unrouted round the
   SOT-23-6.
2. The 3D render does not show copper pours. I could not see whether the
   power polygons existed.
3. Stitching vias I placed came back twice (import-ses re-adds protected
   vias): "drilled holes co-located".
4. Mounting holes: parity warning, the footprint is exclude-from-BOM and the
   symbol was not.
5. **DRC 0, board wrong.** The router ran the sense trace diagonally through
   the VIN polygon and a GND trace through the SW polygon. Each polygon filled
   as two islands joined by a 0.4 mm trace. KiCad accepts that.
6. Exporting the polygons to the router as planes did not help: Freerouting
   stopped routing those nets and still crossed them with others
   (2 unconnected).
7. With protected hand-routed tracks present, Freerouting's own summary
   ("7 unrouted") is wrong; KiCad reported 0 unconnected.

**Tool changes**
- `boardwright/copper.py`: `ZONES` (local polygons, solid pad connection, priority
  over the GND pour), `VIAS`, `TRACKS` (hand routes, fixed before the
  autorouter), `KEEPOUTS`, `TEXT` (silkscreen), all in board-relative mm.
- `POURS = [(net, layers, True)]` for solid (no thermal relief) pours.
- `boardwright build <board> pads` prints every pad's position and net,
  which is what `ZONES` and `TRACKS` are written from.
- `boardwright pins Lib:Symbol [Lib:Footprint]` prints pins and pads.
- Copper plots (`out/F_Cu.png`, `out/B_Cu.png`) in the gate.
- `boardwright/review.py`, part of `verify`: every `ZONES` polygon must fill as one
  piece; any foreign net routed through a polygon is named with a location;
  `REVIEW` in `board.py` declares pad-to-pad distance limits, net length
  limits and pair skew.
- `gen` prints Freerouting's unrouted count; `verify` fails on a `drc.json`
  older than the board.
- `c.add(..., bom=False)` for mechanical parts.

**Method that worked:** join every pad of the power loop by hand (`TRACKS`),
lay the polygons over them (`ZONES`), stitch GND next to each return
(`VIAS`), and leave only the small signals to the autorouter. The sense line
is hand-routed round the quiet side on the top layer so the bottom GND plane
stays whole under the power stage.

**Left open**
- No current-capacity or thermal calculation; polygon widths are by judgement.
- Input loop area is not measured, only pad distances.
- The SW node runs under the input capacitor body to keep the loop tight.
  TI's own layout example does not do that; worth a second opinion.
- Not built. Regulation, ripple and thermal behaviour are unverified.
